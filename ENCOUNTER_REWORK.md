# Revision 32: finite whale visits, rock impacts and gentle contact

A shuffled nonrepeating bag chooses six arrival / resting / departure profiles. The resting offsets, whale scale, bank, yaw, tail phase, stroke amplitude, tempo and left/right pectoral asymmetry differ. Visits have a three-second arrival, six seconds beside the girl and a 2.8-second departure. Monotonic wall time prevents a slow frame rate from extending the visit indefinitely; deterministic simulated time also supports review. Active visits cannot be restarted by pressing summon repeatedly.

The whale nose sweeps against actual visible freestanding rock geometry, including foreground/boulder scenery and spawned obstacles. A struck rock is hidden immediately, emits twenty-four instanced 3D fragments and a bubble burst, and no longer injures the girl. Spawned rocks are disposed by the entity lifecycle. Scenery is restored on a new game. Continuous canyon walls are retained rather than deleting entire cliff tiles. Debris has bounded capacity and lifetime.

A 3,251-vertex skinned body shell sampled from the authored whale grid and the actual pectoral/fluke surfaces refit with the live bone pose. The shell closes the small end rings and adds a 9.5 cm separation allowance. Fifteen girl body/limb spheres, including hands and feet, are projected outside those surfaces; a small impulse occurs on fresh touch. This is kinematic separation, not a fluid or deformable-body simulation. Hair and cloth do not have whale surface constraints. Contact does not damage stamina.

A fresh contact emits three small hearts and adds five affection points (capped at 100). Separation and cooldown gates prevent per-frame farming; each visit permits at most three awards. The HUD shows affection. New game clears hearts, affection and fragments, restores broken scenery and hides the whale.

Browser checks cover six actual rendered poses / destinations, fragment and bubble production, background rock restoration, far-rock noncontact, contact without damage, capped affection and duplicate suppression.
